# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Node operators are the primary audience. They arrive with enough Bitcoin context to care about consensus rules, deployment mechanics, and operational risk, but they need the site to quickly answer whether BIP-110 is legitimate, what it changes, what it does not change, and how to act if they choose to support it.

Secondary audiences include Bitcoin developers, technically sophisticated users, and public readers trying to understand why arbitrary data storage is being challenged at the consensus layer. These readers need a clear argument, visible tradeoffs, and links into the proposal without the page becoming a protocol paper.

## Product Purpose

BIP-110 is a public advocacy and implementation site for a temporary consensus soft fork that limits arbitrary data storage in Bitcoin. The site exists to make the proposal understandable, credible, and actionable: explain the motivation, preserve nuance around tradeoffs, direct users to the full BIP, and help node operators run software that signals support.

Success means a capable node operator can decide what BIP-110 does, understand the activation path and safety claims, find the right installation path, and trust that the site is precise rather than promotional.

## Brand Personality

Principled, exact, calm.

The voice should be sober and technical. It should communicate conviction without crypto hype, explain risks without theatrical urgency, and let credibility come from precision, restraint, and clear boundaries. Copy should be direct, specific, and careful with claims.

## Anti-references

Avoid crypto-hype aesthetics: neon-on-black, trader dashboards, speculative language, meme-token energy, countdown urgency, inflated promises, and decorative Web3 tropes.

Avoid generic SaaS landing-page patterns when they weaken credibility: soft gradient hero stacks, repeated icon cards, vague value propositions, oversized metrics, and polish that feels detached from protocol reality.

Avoid making the site feel like an academic paper as the dominant mode. Technical depth matters, but the primary experience should remain navigable and actionable for node operators.

## Design Principles

Lead with operational confidence. Important actions, dates, compatibility claims, and installation paths should be easy to find and verify.

Show technical seriousness through structure. Use hierarchy, exact copy, source links, and careful tradeoff framing instead of decorative complexity.

Keep the argument legible under disagreement. The design should make room for objections, safety concerns, and temporary limits without burying them or turning them into marketing gloss.

Respect Bitcoin's culture without costume. The interface can be sparse, durable, and protocol-minded, but it should not rely on obvious crypto symbols or faux-terminal aesthetics.

Make action feel reversible and informed. Users should understand what support means, what activation changes, and where uncertainty remains before they are asked to run anything.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA. Preserve keyboard access, visible focus states, readable type sizes, sufficient contrast in light and dark themes, reduced-motion accommodations, and copy that does not require insider shorthand where a precise explanation would do better.
